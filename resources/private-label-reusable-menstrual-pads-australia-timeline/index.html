Private-label production planning

Reusable Menstrual Pad Production Timeline for Australia

For a 500–1,000 piece private-label order with day and overnight sizes, one colour and custom packaging, treat 45–50 days as a rough planning estimate only: the stated production estimate is 25–30 days after the specification and order are finalized, followed by roughly 20 days for a commonly chosen shipping method to Australia.

Planning figure, not a delivery promise

The estimate combines 25–30 days of production after design, logo and packaging are finalized and the order/payment are confirmed, plus about 20 days of shipping for a commonly chosen Australia-bound method. It does not include an established sample schedule or a separately confirmed customs, tax or delivery buffer. Ask the supplier to confirm the start dates and current freight service in writing before setting a launch date.

The order clock runs through distinct milestones

For a startup planning day and overnight reusable pads in one colour with custom packaging, the calendar depends on decisions being closed in sequence. A buyer can move preparation forward while discussing a quote, but an early conversation or an unapproved artwork file does not start the quoted production estimate.

  1. Define the order brief. Specify 500–1,000 pieces, target quantities for day and overnight sizes, one colour, logo treatment and packaging request, plus the Australian delivery postcode. This is preparation time; no duration is confirmed for it.
  2. Resolve the MOQ and specification. The seller quoted an MOQ starting at 500 pieces, but has not confirmed whether that minimum can be combined across the two sizes or applies to each style. Get the counted unit, style split and any packaging minimum in writing before treating the quantity as accepted.
  3. Approve design, logo and packaging. Close the product and artwork details and approve the packaging design. The production estimate starts only after these items are finalized, alongside a confirmed order and payment. No separate artwork or packaging lead time has been quoted.
  4. Confirm the order and payment. Have the accepted specification, quantity by style, price, payment trigger and production start event recorded on the order documents. The estimate is tied to a confirmed order/payment; confirm exactly which event the seller uses.
  5. Production: estimated 25–30 days. This is the seller’s estimate from the stated finalized-design and confirmed-order/payment point. Ask whether the period ends at factory completion, inspection, packing or cargo readiness; that endpoint has not been established here.
  6. Shipping to Australia: roughly 20 days. This is an approximate transit planning input for a commonly chosen method, after shipment readiness. The specific freight method, pickup and delivery scope, routing, customs handling and whether the figure means transit or door delivery are not confirmed.

Adding the quoted production estimate to the approximate shipping estimate gives a rough 45–50 day planning total once the production start conditions are met. It is not a guaranteed arrival window and does not account for time before design approval, unquoted sample work, production scheduling changes, freight booking, customs, taxes or last-mile variations.

Ask what the 500-piece minimum counts

“Starting at 500 pieces” is not enough to plan a two-style order. Ask whether day and overnight sizes can share the MOQ, whether the count applies per style or per order, and whether any size, colour or custom element changes it. Do not assume the 500–1,000 total is accepted as a combined quantity until the seller confirms the split.

Also separate the pad minimum from any custom packaging quantity. Packaging type, print method and production minimum have not been confirmed for this project. Request written confirmation of which components can be ordered at the planned quantity and whether packaging has a different lead-time dependency.

Sample review may sit before the stated production clock

No sample schedule or sample cost is confirmed. If you need to assess the day and overnight sizes, logo placement or packaging before bulk production, ask what sample versions will be made, what each is intended to verify, who approves them and how revisions affect the calendar. Add the supplier’s sample and review dates to your own plan; do not assume they are included in the 25–30 days.

Record approval against a specific sample, artwork and packaging revision. If any approved element changes, ask whether the production start point moves and whether new approval is needed. See the sample process for a planned 500-piece order and the sample approval guide for a practical version-control approach.

Freight choice can change the delivery estimate

The roughly 20-day figure is not a confirmed service commitment. Ask the supplier to identify the proposed method, route, the event from which transit is counted, and whether the estimate ends at port, a named Australian address or another point. Request the quote’s included and excluded charges and customs responsibilities. Freight cost, customs duty, tax and clearance treatment are not established for this order and should not be guessed from the transit estimate.

If you have a launch deadline, request an accelerated freight option as a separate quote. Ask for its estimated transit, price, service scope, cutoff and cargo-ready requirements alongside the commonly chosen option. Faster transport can change freight cost and may not remove production, booking, customs or delivery dependencies; compare the quoted service details rather than assuming a faster mode guarantees an arrival date.

RFQ checklist for a comparable written schedule

  • Day and overnight pad styles, target quantity for each, and confirmation whether the 500-piece MOQ is combined or per style.
  • One colour and the requested logo method, artwork file status and approval contact.
  • Custom packaging type, artwork status, any packaging MOQ and whether it is included in the production schedule.
  • Whether samples are required, sample versions and charges, approval steps, revision timing and shipment plan.
  • Written production start trigger, estimated duration, completion endpoint and dependencies for the 25–30 day estimate.
  • Proposed standard and accelerated freight services to the Australian postcode, with estimated transit start/end events and quoted cost.
  • Carton count, dimensions and weights when available; included services, exclusions, customs clearance and tax responsibilities.
  • Quote validity, payment milestones, order confirmation event and how delays or specification changes affect the schedule.

For packaging decisions, review individual packaging options and the pouch and gift-box guide. The MOQ, sample cost and lead-time guide explains which terms need project-specific confirmation. Send the consolidated request through the reusable-pad RFQ form.

Keep the launch date conditional until dependencies are closed

For planning, put the 45–50 days after the confirmed production start as a provisional window, then add the time needed to finish design, packaging and any sample decisions. Before announcing an arrival date, ask the supplier and freight provider to confirm the schedule against the final order, destination and service. Recheck the estimate if quantity, style split, artwork, packaging or shipping option changes.
