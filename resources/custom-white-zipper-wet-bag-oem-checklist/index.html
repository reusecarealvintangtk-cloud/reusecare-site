Custom White Zipper Wet Bags: OEM Sample Checklist

Buyer request example only: a plain white wet bag with a zipper, approximately 30 × 40 cm, a white snap wrist strap, and PUL or TPU-laminated polyester. Machine washability is a buyer requirement to validate.

ReuseCareNovember 21, 2026

The documented ReuseCare sample is 20 × 25 cm, with one white snap, no zipper and a single-colour print. It is a separate construction. Nothing here establishes that the proposed zipper variant has been produced, verified, stocked or tested.

1. Draw the bag and measure the opening

Issue a dated, numbered drawing with front, back, inside and side views. Mark 300 mm wide × 400 mm high as the nominal finished body size, laid flat and unstretched, excluding the wrist strap. Mark the measurement points and propose a tolerance, such as ±5 mm on each dimension, for the supplier to accept or revise. Show gusset depth if needed, zipper setback, strap anchor position and artwork offsets from finished seams.

Dimension the clear opening separately: measure between the points that actually limit entry when the slider is fully open. A 300 mm body does not guarantee a 300 mm opening. Set a minimum clear span using the largest intended folded item. Have the reviewer insert and remove it without forcing it past the slider or pulling on the seams.

2. Specify each assembly

For the white zipper, list the tape, coil or teeth, slider and pull separately; state which parts must match the approved white swatch. Specify chain type and size, end stops and how the zipper ends are enclosed. For the wrist strap, specify finished width, closed loop size, strap material, snap material and anchor reinforcement. Draw the snap on the strap so it cannot be mistaken for the bag closure.

Request the polyester face weight, laminate polymer and thickness, which side faces inward, and whether the inside is the laminate itself or a separate lining. Ask for separate construction notes if quoting PUL and TPU-laminated polyester as alternatives. Specify seam allowance, stitch and thread, edge finishing and whether seams are sealed. Identify needle holes and zipper ends as test locations: a laminated panel does not establish that the finished assembly resists leakage.

3. Agree on finished-bag pass criteria

Leakage: As a proposed damp-load check, add 100 mL of water to an absorbent cloth, place it inside and close the zipper fully. Put the bag on dry absorbent paper for 30 minutes on its base and 30 minutes on its zipper edge, using a fresh sheet each time. Agree on cloth size and bag loading in advance. Pass if neither sheet shows wet marks and no droplets appear at the seams, strap anchor or zipper ends. Record the orientation of any failure. This is not a standing-water or immersion claim.

Machine washing: Agree on the exact cycle, closure position, detergent, spin and drying method before testing. One proposed screen is five gentle washes at 30 °C with mild detergent, zipper closed and line drying after each wash. After the final dry, remeasure the body and clear opening and repeat the leakage check. Pass if the bag still meets the drawing limits, the zipper and snap each complete ten open-close cycles without sticking or separating, and there is no seam opening, laminate lifting, visible colour transfer or print peeling. Agree on the number of finished units and require each to pass; record results by unit.

4. Sign off a numbered printed sample

Label the actual printed unit with its sample ID, drawing revision, material codes and artwork revision. Check placement against the drawing and compare the print and zipper white to approved references under the same lighting. Keep an unwashed signed reference and run wash and leakage checks on separately numbered units made to the same revision. Record exceptions, who approved them and the approval date. Require a revised sample if a critical component or print process changes before bulk production.

5. Request comparable price tiers

Ask for 300, 500 and 1,000 pieces as comparison tiers, not as stated minimums. Have the supplier mark unavailable tiers and specify the actual minimum per material, artwork and version. Keep construction and packaging constant across tiers. Separate unit price, setup, printed sample fee and any credit, testing, packaging and freight. Request sample and bulk lead times separately, with currency, trade term and named place, carton quantities and quote validity. List substitutions and exclusions explicitly so a lower price cannot hide a change to the lining or zipper.
