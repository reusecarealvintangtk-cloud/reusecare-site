PUL Lamination Process

How fabric and film feed, line monitoring and roll identification are kept connected before material is released for product sampling or bulk use.

Prepared by ReuseCareReview owner: Materials & production teamUpdated October 7, 20267 minute read
Buyer takeaway

A PUL fabric description is not enough by itself. Before production approval, the team needs a controlled material reference, an identified run and a review record that states what was checked and who accepted it.

1. Confirm the material reference before the line starts

Start from the agreed base fabric, film or laminate specification, colour or print reference, approved sample revision and order identity. The production record should make it possible to trace a finished roll back to the materials and instructions used at the start of that run. This is especially important when a buyer has approved a particular hand feel, print or construction.

Material identityRecord the incoming fabric and film references, supplier or lot identifiers where used, and the order or sample revision.
Run settingsKeep the line settings and any approved operating range in the controlled production record, not only in informal notes.
Visual checkpointsDefine the appearance, alignment, edge condition and defect observations that trigger review.
Roll traceabilityMark finished rolls so the material can be linked to the completed review and any follow-up action.

2. Feed fabric and film without losing alignment

Fabric and film must enter the line with the agreed orientation and without avoidable distortion. The operator watches feed consistency, edge alignment and visible surface condition as the material moves forward. A clear process instruction identifies the points to stop, adjust or isolate material for review; a generic statement that a line is “running normally” is not a usable approval record.

3. Review the finished roll against the agreed reference

After lamination, the team reviews the roll and its identification against the order record. The review can include appearance, obvious marks, edge condition, roll labelling and any approved test or sample requirement. The exact acceptance criteria depend on the agreed construction and end use, so this page does not assign universal pass values or claim a test result for every PUL material.

4. Keep the approval evidence usable

  • Product, material and sample revision reference.
  • Incoming material and finished-roll identification.
  • Line and inspection record for the production run.
  • Any agreed test method, result record or sample observation.
  • Reviewer role, review date and decision.
  • Clear treatment of any hold, rework or follow-up requirement.

For reusable diapers, wet bags, bibs and other PUL applications, the final product requirement may include steps beyond fabric lamination. A fabric review does not automatically prove the performance of seams, elastic, snaps or a finished item after washing. Link the lamination record to the actual product sample and inspection plan.
