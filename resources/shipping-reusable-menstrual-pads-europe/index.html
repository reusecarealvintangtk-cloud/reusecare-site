Freight and quote planning

Shipping Reusable Menstrual Pads to Europe

Compare shipping scenarios from the same shipment facts: final quantity, packed carton count, dimensions and weights, pickup point, destination postcode, delivery terms and customs responsibility. Freight is only one part of landed cost.

Compare like with like

A sea, air, express or consolidated service quote is meaningful only when it uses the same ready date, carton data, pickup and delivery points, service scope and named Incoterm. Ask for a separate goods figure and freight figure, then have the EU importer or broker estimate import charges from confirmed customs inputs.

Start with the packed shipment, not a guessed weight

Reusable menstrual pads are compact products, but presentation packaging, individual packs, inserts, master cartons and protective materials all affect the final shipment. Do not estimate freight from piece count alone. Ask production to confirm the approved packing configuration and record how many retail units fit in each master carton, the carton count, outer dimensions and actual gross and net weight.

If a pack design or carton has not been finalized, label the information as provisional. Request an updated freight quote after packing is confirmed. Changes to the number of pads per retail pack, packaging thickness or carton layout can change both chargeable volume and handling assumptions. Use the same packing revision across quotes so a comparison does not accidentally pair different shipments.

Send one complete set of quote inputs

  • Product styles, total pieces, pack quantity and any sample cartons.
  • Carton count, outer length/width/height, gross weight and net weight.
  • Pickup city or facility and the buyer’s full destination postcode and country.
  • Required delivery location: commercial address, residential address, warehouse, port or another named point.
  • Target cargo-ready date and whether the shipment can be split.
  • Preferred service options, insurance request and any delivery appointment constraints.
  • Proposed Incoterm, named place and the party responsible for export, main carriage, import clearance and final delivery.

For a buyer comparing an initial 500-piece order, ask the factory to quote the agreed product quantity and packaging separately from sample charges and freight. Do not infer that a small order will use a particular mode or arrive by a particular date; carrier availability, packing, customs and destination service all affect the result.

Compare service scope and schedule carefully

Ask each provider to state what its price includes: pickup, export handling, line-haul transport, terminal handling, customs brokerage, import charges, last-mile delivery, residential or remote-area fees, fuel or peak surcharges, insurance and any storage or re-delivery charges. These items vary by service and contract. A low headline freight number may cover a narrower route or leave local handling and clearance outside the quote.

Record the schedule as separate stages: production completion, packing and cargo readiness, pickup, transit estimate, customs presentation and clearance, and final delivery. Request the quote date, validity window, service name and assumptions. Transit estimates are not delivery guarantees unless the carrier’s terms expressly provide one. Ask how delays, customs inspection, incomplete consignee details or a missed delivery appointment are handled.

For a sample shipment, request a separate quote using the sample’s actual packaging and courier destination. Do not simply divide the bulk freight amount by pieces: parcel services, minimum charges, dimensional weight and customs processing can make sample economics different from a consolidated cargo shipment.

Keep customs and taxes in the importer’s calculation

EU import guidance explains that customs authorities rely on the nature, origin and value of the goods. The product’s classification affects the applicable duties and rules; the customs declaration also informs any duties, VAT or tariffs due. Therefore, no exact customs total can be guaranteed without the confirmed classification, origin, customs value, freight, delivery terms and importer setup.

Ask the buyer’s customs representative to confirm the proposed CN classification and review current official systems. The European Commission’s Access2Markets My Trade Assistant can help investigate trade requirements and tariff information for a defined product and origin. The importer should confirm the result with the competent customs authority or its broker, and verify who will use the EORI and lodge the declaration.

Write down which quote is for freight only and which estimate includes import duty, VAT or clearance. A seller quoting “door delivery” does not automatically mean every tax, fee or importer obligation is included. Read the Incoterm, named place and service conditions. If a delivered-duty-paid option is proposed, the importer should have a qualified representative confirm who acts as importer, how the declaration is made and which charges are included.

Use a simple comparison sheet

Place quotes side by side with columns for provider and service, quote date and validity, cargo-ready assumptions, carton count and dimensions, chargeable weight or volume, route, delivery point, estimated transit, included handling, exclusions, insurance, customs broker, import charges, final-mile terms and total stated by the provider. Add a separate importer-confirmed estimate for customs duty and VAT. This makes unclear assumptions visible before purchase approval.

Before paying a deposit, confirm the consignee’s legal name, address, phone and tax or EORI details requested by the broker. Agree who supplies the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document and any origin or inspection documents required for the shipment. Match descriptions and quantities across documents to the approved product revision. The importer should review the documents before dispatch, not only after the cargo arrives.

Official sources checked

  • Your Europe: customs duties and import procedures, including classification and declaration inputs.
  • European Commission: EORI guidance.
  • European Commission: Access2Markets My Trade Assistant.
  • Belgian Customs: EORI application information.
  • Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011: textile fibre names and labelling; Your Europe textile label guidance.
  • European Commission GPSR summary and official Regulation (EU) 2023/988 text.
  • ECHA: REACH Annex XVII restricted substances.

Important: This is general freight and import information, not legal, customs, tax or logistics advice. The EU or Belgian importer must confirm product classification, duties, VAT, responsible economic operator, label language requirements and current compliance with authorities or qualified advisers. Customs costs and delivery dates cannot be guaranteed without shipment-specific facts and carrier terms. No CE-marking requirement or product-compliance claim is made here.

Frequently asked questions

Can a supplier quote the complete landed cost to Europe?

A supplier may quote goods and a defined freight service, but total landed costs depend on classification, origin, customs value, freight, Incoterm, VAT treatment and importer setup. The importer or broker must confirm the final estimate.

What information is needed for a useful freight comparison?

Provide the pickup location, European destination postcode, quantity, carton count, dimensions, gross and net weights, shipment readiness date, delivery service and named Incoterm and place.

Is express air always the fastest delivered option?

Not necessarily. Transit estimates exclude or vary with production readiness, pickup, export handling, customs clearance, delivery area and carrier operations. Compare the service definition and quoted schedule rather than relying on a mode label.

Who confirms customs duty and VAT?

The importer or its customs representative confirms classification, origin, customs value, duty, VAT and clearance charges with current official tools and the competent authorities.
