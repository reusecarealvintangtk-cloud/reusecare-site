Wholesale assortment planning

Period Underwear Wholesale Six-Pack Planning Guide

Convert a six-piece assortment idea into a size-by-color schedule, a packaging brief and a comparable wholesale quote.

Prepared by ReuseCareNovember 19, 2026
Request a sample or quote →Read the buyer guide

Decide what a shopper receives

A six-piece pack is an assortment planning example. The photos show one style across colorways, and a pack offer would need to be confirmed in the quotation. Start with the channel and the buying decision: a shopper might select a size and receive six colors, or select a size and receive a repeated set of core colors. Decide whether the color combination will be fixed or whether you want to request several pack variants. A fixed recipe simplifies the listing and tells the packing team exactly what belongs in every unit.

Use the period underwear reference gallery and filtered product catalog to describe your starting point. List the colors by reference code and request physical swatches or sample garments before approving any color name or product image for the pack.

Translate pack counts into garment counts

Build a sheet with one row for each proposed pack variant and size. Give each color its own column and enter the number of pieces of that color in the pack. Require the color columns to sum to six on every row. Multiply each color count by the number of packs planned for that row. The resulting totals tell the supplier what to make and tell your receiving team what to reconcile.

For example, 100 packs in one planned size use 600 garments. With one piece each of six colors, that means 100 garments per color in that size. With two pieces of color A, two of color B and one each of colors C and D, the same pack count calls for 200 each of A and B and 100 each of C and D. These figures illustrate the calculation. Send your own size curve and have the supplier confirm which sizes and combinations can be quoted.

Plan for short colors and replenishment

Ask how minimums apply across garment colors, sizes, pack variants and printed packaging. A shortage in a color that appears in every pack can hold up the entire assembly. Decide whether to order spare components, accept a partial run or authorize a specific alternate recipe. Document any approved alternate and check whether its retail description, identifier or artwork also has to change.

Ask the supplier to quote replenishment against the same size and color schedule. Confirm whether additional garments and packaging can be ordered on terms that suit a repeat run. When reviewing your inventory plan, count garments and sellable packs separately so an incomplete pack does not enter available inventory.

Wholesale pack checklist

  • Provide the garment reference, desired size curve, pack count by variant and calculated piece count by size and color.
  • Approve a pack recipe and specify fold order, whether pieces need individual labels or wraps, and how staff identify the size.
  • Confirm retail identifier placement and scannability, outer carton markings, units per carton and any mixed-carton rules with your receiving team.
  • Ask for garment, assembly and packaging charges, any setup costs, project-specific minimums and lead time assumptions.
  • Give the destination, requested shipment window and the delivery terms needed to compare offers.

Review a packed sample before ordering

Send the schedule and packaging brief through the period underwear sample and quote form. Request a garment sample for fit review and a packed sample for checking presentation and assembly. Ask the supplier to quote your preferred recipe and identify any effects on cost or timing if you change the color mix or package format. For printed packaging or branded labels, use the private-label process to provide artwork and request proofs.

Open the packed sample as a shopper would. Count the pieces, compare each size label and color with the recipe, and confirm the pack description matches the contents. Ask the receiving team to check the carton details against its intake requirements. Record the approved garment reference, pack recipe and artwork version together so purchase orders and reorders point to the same specification.
