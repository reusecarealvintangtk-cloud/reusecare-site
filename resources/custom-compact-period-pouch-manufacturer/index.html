Custom Compact Period Pouch Manufacturer: 12 × 12 × 4 cm OEM Design Checklist

Planning a 12 × 12 × 4 cm pouch with an internal zipped compartment and outer sleeve pocket? Use this checklist to brief a manufacturer and agree what a first sample must demonstrate.

ReuseCareNovember 26, 2026

This buyer checklist uses a proposed 12 × 12 × 4 cm period pouch with a waterproof-lined internal zip compartment and a separate outer sleeve pocket. It also covers how to request reusable menstrual pads, wet bags, private-label packaging, and design and sample support. This pouch is a proposed design; ReuseCare has not confirmed it as manufactured, tested or in stock.

1. Draw the two pockets and define how to measure them

Send front, side and interior views, plus a cross-section showing the divider, lining and zipper. Start with 120 mm wide × 120 mm high × 40 mm deep as the proposed finished exterior dimensions. Ask the supplier to mark where each dimension is taken with the empty pouch relaxed and the gusset unfolded without stretching. Agree tolerances before sampling and specify whether zipper pulls and trims count toward the overall size.

Show the internal zip compartment independently of any main opening. Mark the zipper position, minimum clear opening between the stops, usable internal width and depth, and clearance around the slider. On the outside, specify the sleeve opening, insertion depth and whether it stays open or needs retention. Check fit with both pockets loaded: a full sleeve can squeeze the lined compartment even when each pocket fits on its own. Provide the folded measurements of the largest intended pad and name what the sleeve is meant to hold.

2. Specify the lining without promising a waterproof bag

Ask for the proposed shell fabric, lining construction, laminate type and orientation, zipper tape and slider, seam finish, and reinforcement at the sleeve opening. Request swatches and a sketch showing how the liner joins the shell. If a supplier proposes a TPU-laminated fabric or a different lining, ask how it changes thickness, usable space, care instructions and assembly.

Waterproof lining is a material requirement for the internal compartment. It does not establish that the zipper, stitched seams or assembled compartment prevent leakage, and it does not apply to the outer sleeve or the whole bag. Review the existing wet bag range for construction references and use the custom wet bag RFQ checklist to frame questions about closures and seams. Neither substitutes for evaluating the proposed pouch.

Decide whether the sleeve is for a clean spare or for an accessory. If it carries a clean pad, specify that the sleeve must stay dry during the agreed leakage check with the inner compartment loaded. That gives the sample reviewer a clear way to evaluate the divider.

3. Agree fit, leak and wash criteria before sampling

  • Fit: Use the largest intended folded pad and the expected sleeve contents. Propose that the pad pass through the opening without force, the zip close without catching fabric, and the sleeve contents stay in place under agreed handling.
  • Leakage: Agree the test fluid or damp load, quantity, orientation and hold time. Set a pass criterion for visible transfer onto a dry indicator at seams, zipper ends and the divider. Record which areas were checked; a pass under agreed conditions is not a whole-bag waterproof rating.
  • Wash: Agree a wash and dry method and number of cycles for the intended care instructions. Afterwards, recheck dimensions, zip operation and leakage, and inspect for lining separation, puckering or colour transfer.

Give the drawing, material list and sample the same revision identifier. Record measurements, photos and pass/fail results against that revision. If the lining, zipper, pattern or seam construction changes, issue an updated revision and repeat affected checks. Approve the final sample and packaging proof in writing before authorising bulk production.

4. Compare quotes by category and verify who makes what

Request separate quote lines for the pouch, reusable menstrual pads, wet bags and private-label packaging, plus an optional assembled-kit line. For each, compare the quoted specification, quantity breaks, order minimums, unit basis, one-time charges and inclusions. Ask what design support covers, what sample stages are available, and how revisions are charged and scheduled. Use the reusable menstrual pad brand guide to define the pad assortment and packaging before comparing kit prices.

Ask for the full registered name, registration number and address of the contracting entity, supported by its business registration. Request the actual factory name and site address for each category, who handles sampling and bulk production, and which processes are subcontracted. Ask for factory registration and, where the supplier and factory differ, documentation of the relationship. Check that quote and invoice details match the contracting entity and that any supporting reports apply to the relevant site, material or product. The manufacturer and partner-factory guide explains what to compare.
