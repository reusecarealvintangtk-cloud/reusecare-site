How Samples Are Approved Before Bulk Production

A practical approval path that turns a reusable textile sample into a clear, controlled reference before bulk materials are cut.

Prepared by ReuseCareReview owner: Product & quality review teamUpdated October 7, 20268 minute read
Buyer takeaway

An approved sample is not simply a photo marked “okay.” It is a named product revision with measurable details, a responsible reviewer and a clear rule for what may proceed into bulk production.

Use two controlled sample stages

A development sample is used to resolve construction, materials, dimensions, print direction and packaging intent. When those points are agreed, a pre-production sample can be used as the final production reference. The second stage matters because it connects the approved design to the selected bulk materials, components and production instructions.

Development sampleConfirms the product concept, layer order, components, measurements and visual direction.
Pre-production sampleConfirms the approved revision against the intended bulk material and process.
Approval ownerRecords the buyer or authorized reviewer role, decision date and requested corrections.
Production releaseLinks the signed sample revision to the production order, quality brief and packing instructions.

What belongs in the sample-approval record

The record should be detailed enough that the buyer, product team and inspection team are referring to the same item. For reusable menstrual pads, cloth diapers, nursing pads, bibs, wet bags and fabric programs, the relevant fields can include:

  • Product name, style code, sample revision and approval date.
  • Finished dimensions and agreed tolerances for critical points.
  • Layer order, material names, colors or prints and component positions.
  • Seam, binding, elastic, snap, label and trim requirements where applicable.
  • Artwork file revision, scale, orientation and placement reference.
  • Functional checks required for the intended use, with method and acceptance rule.
  • Retail pack contents, barcode or label artwork, carton quantity and carton marks.

A reference image is helpful, but it cannot replace these written details. Two samples can look similar in a photo while using a different inner layer, elastic length, material weight or label position.

Assign a reviewer and a decision

Every approval should name a responsible role. The buyer may approve the market-facing look, label and packaging; the product or quality reviewer checks that measurements, construction and inspection criteria are sufficiently defined. If a correction is needed, mark the sample as revise rather than leaving an informal comment that can be missed later.

The approval record should state one of three statuses: approved for the next sample stage, revise and resubmit, or approved for bulk production. Each status should include the date, reviewer role and the sample revision being decided.

Carry the approved sample into production control

Before cutting, verify that the material, print or color, components and packing artwork match the approved revision. During sewing and inspection, keep the sample record available beside the workmanship and measurement checks. This makes it easier to catch a changed component or placement before goods are packed.

Do not add important requirements after approval

Waterproof performance, wash requirements, laboratory testing, market-specific labelling and packaging content should be defined before the bulk-production release. Adding them only after goods are finished can create a disagreement about whether the request was part of the approved product specification.

A concise buyer sign-off checklist

  • Is the sample revision number visible and matched to the order?
  • Are critical measurements and tolerances agreed?
  • Are materials, layer order and components identified?
  • Are print, label and packaging revisions identified?
  • Are functional tests and acceptance rules named where needed?
  • Is the reviewer role, decision and date recorded?
  • Does the production and inspection brief point to the same approved revision?

A controlled approval record protects both the buyer and supplier: it creates one shared source of truth before bulk production begins.
