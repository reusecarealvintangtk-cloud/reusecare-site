Buyer test protocol

Period Underwear Gusset Absorbency Testing

Specify a repeatable test for the finished garment before comparing samples or approving performance wording.

Prepared by ReuseCareNovember 19, 2026
Request a sample or quote →Read the buyer guide

Identify the sample and the claim

Begin with the decision the test must support. A sourcing team may want to rank prototypes, while a quality team may need an acceptance rule for a production inspection. Write down the intended use and proposed performance wording before selecting a method. For a comparison, ask every supplier to test the same specimen type under the same conditions. For a proposed label claim, agree on the evidence needed for that wording and destination market.

The white gusset surface visible in the photos identifies an inspection area. Its color establishes neither fiber identity nor absorbency. Request the layer order, exact composition by layer and gusset dimensions for the specified sample. Select your reference from the period underwear page or filtered catalog and assign each physical sample a revision, size and color code before testing.

Agree on how to apply the liquid

Ask the supplier or testing lab to propose a documented method that reflects the intended comparison. Specify the liquid and its preparation, conditioning of the garment, test orientation and support, dosing location, dose volume, delivery rate and time between applications. Decide whether a single application or repeated doses are needed. Mark the application point on a diagram so reviewers can reproduce its distance from the seams.

Agree on the number of specimens per condition and the sizes or colors to represent. If you want information about curved fit or tension, describe the fixture and how the garment will be held. A flat panel test answers a narrower question than a finished garment test that includes gusset seams. Record specimen preparation and fixture details in the report so the results remain interpretable.

Define what the reviewer records

  • For intake, agree on how to time the disappearance of surface liquid and how to record pooling across repeated doses.
  • For retained liquid, specify the weighing points, any drain period and how to account for runoff. Define the endpoint before discussing a capacity figure.
  • For surface wetness, ask how the lab will measure transfer to a contact material under an agreed load and contact time.
  • For leakage, identify the areas to inspect, including the underside, leg openings and seams. Define visible wetting or transfer criteria and record the dose and location of any event.
  • For consistency, request individual specimen results and the rules for handling missing measurements, retests and failed units.

Plan the before-and-after wash comparison

If the buyer needs evidence after laundering, specify the cycle count, wash settings, detergent, drying method and conditioning after drying. Ask the lab how it will allocate specimens across unwashed and washed groups and ensure comparable construction. Have reviewers record gusset distortion or seam changes before dosing. Define the acceptance rule for each condition so a favorable initial result cannot mask a failure after washing.

Agree in advance whether all units must meet each criterion or whether a stated sampling rule applies. Ask for raw observations alongside any summary, including photographs when they help locate leakage. Log method departures and obtain agreement before combining data from different setups. Keep measured outcomes with the exact specimen and method. A volume reported under one method should only be compared with another volume once the relevant conditions are aligned.

Put testing into the sample and quote request

Attach your draft protocol to the sample and quote request. Ask which measurements can be made during development, which require a lab, who supplies the specimens and what fees and reporting times apply. Request enough units for destructive evaluation and a retained reference. Review the proposed method before sample dispatch so everyone knows how to identify and prepare the garments.

Use the private-label workflow to coordinate any artwork that depends on the results. Keep the proposed claim pending until the report is reviewed against the agreed criteria. If the gusset build changes, document the change and agree which tests to repeat before treating that revision as approved.
