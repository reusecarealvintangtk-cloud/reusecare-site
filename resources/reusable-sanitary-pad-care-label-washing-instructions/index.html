Reusable Sanitary Pad Washing Instructions: How Bulk Buyers Approve a Care Label

Approve wash wording against the exact pad construction and an agreed care protocol. A product photo or a generic laundry recipe cannot establish safe instructions for an unverified material combination.

Prepared by ReuseCareUpdated November 8, 20266 minute read
Discuss a product brief →Use the checklist
Direct answer

Bulk buyers should approve a reusable-pad care label only after the supplier identifies the exact SKU and layer composition, agrees the wash and drying fields, and records pre- and post-wash checks against a dated product revision. If physical samples are not required, use dated product and label photos, a technical sheet and written sign-off.

Start with the product revision, not a stock recipe

Reusable sanitary pads can combine different absorbent and waterproof materials, bindings, stitching and fasteners. A care label should therefore describe a defined item. Confirm the style code, finished dimensions, layer order, material name and fiber composition for each layer, plus the snap or other closure. Identify the specification revision that the label covers.

One reported procurement reference describes a 17 × 27 cm pad with three absorbent layers, one waterproof layer, plastic snaps and four pads per OPP bag. In the same exchange, the buyer asked for cotton absorbent material while the supplier reply said “Polyester.” That leaves composition unresolved. Treat the figures as a reference to clarify, not as a final approved specification or a description of the pictured product.

The pink product photographs here illustrate a product style only. They do not prove its composition, layer count, performance, wash tolerance or equivalence to any buyer’s SKU. Keep those facts in the technical sheet and approval record.

Agree each care-label field with the supplier

Write the care method as fields to be decided and verified for this construction. Record the proposed wash temperature and method, detergent guidance, whether bleach or fabric softener is allowed, whether soaking is allowed, and the drying method. Include any handling or storage instruction only when it has a defined basis. These are approval questions, not universal recommendations: no validated program or cycle count is available for the example above.

Where a care field is unsupported or still disputed, leave it open in the approval draft instead of converting a guess into label copy. The supplier and buyer should agree the proposed wording, the test or evidence used to support it, and the acceptance rule before artwork is released. If a change in material or construction could affect care, review the wording again.

Define checks before and after the agreed wash

Set the checks before evaluating a sample so both sides know what passing means. Capture the pad’s condition before washing and compare it after the agreed method. Typical buyer-controlled observations include dimensions, seam integrity, snap operation, layer alignment and visible changes. For waterproofing and absorbency, name the method, sample condition and pass criteria; do not substitute an informal impression for a defined functional check.

Record how many wash cycles are in scope only when the buyer and supplier have agreed that evaluation. Do not imply a cycle life, leak result or absorbency result without evidence for that SKU and method. If the buyer has its own institutional laundering process, share it early so the product and label can be reviewed against the intended use.

Approve artwork and the pack as a separate check

Care wording can be technically clear and still appear incorrectly on the delivered product. Review the final label proof for legibility, language, placement and revision. Compare the physical or documented pack against the order unit: in the reported example, four pads per OPP bag is a buyer reference that needs confirmation. Verify the bag count, pad count, label or insert, barcode and any required language against the approved pack specification.

For buyers who do not require physical samples, request dated photos that show the product, visible layers or construction detail, care label and completed pack. Pair them with the technical sheet and a written statement identifying the SKU and revision being approved. This creates a traceable decision; it does not claim that a photo replaces a physical or functional test when one is required.

Care-label approval checklist

Exact SKU and layersRecord style, dimensions, each layer’s material and composition, construction and closure.
Wash fieldsAgree temperature and method, detergent, bleach, softener, soaking and drying guidance.
Before and afterDefine dimension, seam, snap and layer-alignment observations, plus agreed waterproof and absorbency tests.
Evidence and limitsLink checks to a method, acceptance rule and any stated cycle scope; leave unsupported claims out.
Label and packCheck final wording, language, label revision, pad count, bag type and pack presentation.
Sign-off controlRecord approver, date, product revision, label revision and disposition; reapprove meaningful changes.

Keep the signed record with the approved specification so procurement, production and inspection teams use the same revision. For a sample-free review, retain the dated image set and technical sheet alongside the written sign-off.
