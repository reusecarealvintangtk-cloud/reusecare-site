DDP Shipping and Carton Planning for Bulk Nursing Pads

Translate retail packs into order quantities and master cartons before comparing freight. The current carton reference is 400 four-pad packs in a 53 × 38 × 48 cm carton; verify the packed configuration and obtain a destination-specific DDP quote.

Current packing reference

One retail pack contains four nursing breast pads. The current master-carton reference is 400 retail packs per carton, with carton dimensions of 53 × 38 × 48 cm. That equals 1,600 pads per carton. Treat these as starting figures and confirm them against the approved product and packed shipment.

Begin with the correct product unit

Nursing pads are breast pads worn inside a bra to manage breast milk leakage. They are not menstrual pads. This guide concerns unit conversion, packing assumptions and a freight quote request; it does not establish suitability for another product category. Keep the pack description precise on purchase orders, commercial documents and shipment instructions.

When buyers discuss quantities, “pieces” can mean a single pad, a retail pack, or an outer carton. Put the unit next to every quantity and show the conversion. That simple discipline prevents an order for thousands of pads from being mistaken for thousands of retail packages, or a carton count from being read as a product count.

Carton arithmetic, step by step

The confirmed retail configuration is four pads in each retail pack. The carton reference is 400 retail packs. Multiply packs by pads per pack:

400 retail packs/carton × 4 pads/retail pack = 1,600 pads/carton.

This calculation describes product-unit counts only. It does not establish net weight, gross weight, volume weight, pallet count, container utilization or the exact cubic capacity available after packing materials. Ask the supplier to confirm gross and net weights, carton count, and any palletization details for the actual shipment before booking.

For quantity planning, divide required retail packs by 400 to find the carton equivalent. For example, 800 packs correspond arithmetically to two cartons; 1,000 packs correspond to 2.5 carton equivalents. Half-carton arithmetic does not guarantee that the factory will ship a partially filled carton in that arrangement. Confirm whether order quantities must be whole cartons, whether mixed cartons are permitted, and how any remainder will be packed and labeled.

If the purchase request is stated as individual pads, first divide by four to get retail-pack equivalents, then divide by 400 for carton equivalents. A requested 3,200 pads equals 800 retail packs, or two carton equivalents on the stated assumptions. Have the supplier restate the commercial quantity as pads, retail packs and cartons in the final quote so both parties can spot a unit mismatch.

Verify the packed dimensions before freight booking

The current carton dimensions are 53 × 38 × 48 cm. Their simple rectangular volume is 96,672 cubic centimetres, or 0.096672 cubic metres per carton. This is a geometric calculation from the stated outside dimensions, not a carrier billing weight or a guarantee of warehouse/container utilization. Carriers may use dimensional weight formulas, minimum charges, handling rules and rounding practices that depend on the service and route.

Confirm whether the dimensions are external measurements of a closed shipping carton and whether they apply to the final retail packaging. A packaging revision, insert, protective wrap or carton strength change can affect the result. Request the final carton specification in writing with length, width, height, gross weight and pack count. If palletized, also request loaded pallet dimensions and gross weight; do not infer palletization from a carton figure.

Before approving a packing plan, make sure it matches the approved product revision and four-pad retail pack. Ask for a packing list or carton breakdown showing how many retail packs and individual pads are in each carton. Confirm any assortment by style or color, barcode labels, carton marks and buyer receiving requirements before production begins.

Understand what a current DDP quote needs

DDP is a delivery term whose cost and operational treatment depend on the agreed transaction and destination. A useful current quote requires destination country, full delivery address or at least postal code, shipment quantity, product and packing details, requested timing, and any known delivery constraints. Freight providers may also need pickup location, service level, carton gross weight and dimensions, customs classification information, and whether the goods move by air, sea or another mode.

Freight, duties and taxes can change with destination, routing, shipment date, quantity, carrier, customs treatment and other conditions. Therefore a past freight figure cannot be treated as a current DDP commitment. Request a new written quotation against current destination and postal data, with validity period, included charges, exclusions, delivery assumptions and party responsibilities stated. Ask who will act as importer of record and how import documentation and any additional charges are handled. Review the proposed term with your own shipping and customs advisers where needed.

Do not compare one quote that includes destination delivery and import charges with another that ends at a port or excludes duties as if they were equivalent. Normalize the scope. Request separate product, packaging and transport lines where available; ask whether final-mile delivery, remote-area fees, duties, taxes, inspection charges or other surcharges are included or excluded. This guide does not publish an old freight quote, determine tax liability or guarantee a landed cost.

Align pack, carton and order documentation

Use consistent units across the RFQ, quote, purchase order, pro forma invoice, packing list and shipping instructions. A practical line item may show required retail packs, pads per pack, total pad count, expected cartons and any partial-carton handling. If multiple designs or pack versions are involved, break out quantities by version rather than combining them into a single ambiguous total.

Ask the supplier to identify any assumptions in carton count, packing material, gross weight and shipment mode. If a final pack changes after sample approval, ask for revised carton data before freight is booked. Keep the agreed master carton reference alongside the product specification. For procurement controls and document-scope review, see the institutional procurement checklist; for the product approval record, see nursing-pad sample approval.

Checklist for a freight-ready RFQ

  • Specify the order quantity in retail packs and individual pads.
  • Confirm four pads per retail pack and 400 packs per carton.
  • Use the 53 × 38 × 48 cm carton reference only after confirming final packed dimensions.
  • Request carton gross/net weight, count and pallet data if applicable.
  • Provide current destination and postal code for DDP pricing.
  • Ask for quote validity, included/excluded charges, delivery assumptions and responsibilities.
  • Reconcile pack version, order quantity and packing list before dispatch.

These figures are a current starting specification, not a substitute for shipment-specific confirmation. Have the supplier verify the final packing configuration and quote it in writing before committing to a delivery budget.

Frequently asked questions

How many nursing pads are in one carton?

At four pads per retail pack and 400 retail packs per carton, the arithmetic is 1,600 individual pads per carton.

What are the carton dimensions?

The current reference is 53 × 38 × 48 cm per carton. Confirm these dimensions for the final packed configuration before booking freight.

Can a DDP freight cost be estimated without a postal code?

A current DDP quote needs current destination and postal data, shipment quantity and packing details. Freight, duties and taxes should be re-quoted against those inputs.

Does 400 packs per carton mean 400 pads?

No. The carton reference is 400 retail packs. Each pack contains four pads, so 400 packs represent 1,600 individual pads.

For an accurate, current shipping proposal, send the order quantity, destination country and postal code, and required timing through the nursing-pad RFQ form. Confirm the final specification and packing plan on the approved sample and written quotation.
