Dual-Compartment Period Wet Bags for School and Sport

Specify two useful storage areas, then check the finished sample with the intended contents. Separate linings and openings are construction features; performance needs its own agreed evaluation.

Prepared by ReuseCareNovember 27, 2026
Request a sample and itemised quote →

Define what each compartment must carry

A period-care range for school or sport may need to organise clean products and temporarily carry used reusable items or soiled clothing. Record the intended contents of compartment A and compartment B rather than specifying two pockets without a capacity brief. A small pad pouch and a garment wet bag can serve different purposes within the same range.

ReuseCare confirms that its double-compartment designs have an independent lining and opening for each compartment. Select an actual model or request development drawings before ordering. This guide does not assign dimensions, a waterproof rating or a tested capacity to an unspecified design. The photographed existing bag is an exterior reference rather than evidence of the two-compartment construction.

Draw both openings, linings and the divider

Ask for front, back, interior and cross-section views. Mark the exterior width and height, gusset depth where relevant, zipper or other closure position, minimum clear opening and usable depth for each area. Agree how to measure the relaxed sample and where tolerances apply. External dimensions alone cannot describe usable space after lining and seam allowances.

Show how each lining attaches at its opening and how the divider joins the perimeter. Confirm the material on both sides, whether layers share stitching, and any proposed seam finishing or sealing. Record shell fabric, lining or laminate type, closure components and care requirements by part. An outer sleeve on the compact period pouch concept is a different design from two independently lined compartments.

Check useful capacity with the actual products

Supply the largest intended folded underwear or garment and the expected clean-product load. Define item count and folding method. Load both compartments simultaneously: filling one side may reduce the usable volume of the other. Check that the contents pass through the opening without force and the closures operate without catching the lining.

  • Measure the filled bag thickness and compare it with the intended backpack or sports bag space.
  • Check access to each opening while the other compartment is loaded.
  • Review slider clearance, divider distortion, seam strain and attachment points.
  • Record which model and item sizes were used; do not generalise the result to all clothing sizes.

Use the storage pouch guide to define a smaller clean-product accessory where appropriate. Two compartments are useful only if their capacity and access suit the chosen kit contents.

Agree transfer and finished-bag checks

Independent linings do not alone demonstrate that liquid cannot pass through seams, zipper ends or a divider attachment. Before evaluating a sample, agree the damp load or test fluid, quantity, contact time, bag orientation and observation points. Place an agreed dry indicator in the adjacent compartment and check the exterior separately. Record any transfer and its location.

Define pass criteria in advance for the particular use. A result under an agreed damp-load check does not establish immersion resistance, an odour barrier, antibacterial performance or suitability for every storage duration. Ask what finished-bag evidence is available for the actual construction and distinguish material-level reports from assembly-level checks.

Verify care and everyday handling on samples

Agree the washing and drying method supported by the shell, lining, closures and decoration. Record the cycle count and detergent used for evaluation. Keep an unwashed comparison sample, then inspect for lining separation, shrinkage, colour transfer and print damage after drying. Remeasure capacity-related dimensions and repeat the agreed opening and damp-transfer checks.

Inspect closure ends, lining attachment and strap stitching where a strap is included. Agree any repeated-use or load evaluation with the supplier; no cycle count or strength result is claimed here. Care instructions and product copy should reflect the approved construction and evidence.

Confirm order route and approve one revision

Most ReuseCare products have ready-stock options, with existing styles available from 50 pieces subject to the selected model and inventory. Ask whether the double-compartment style you need is available. Logo printing, woven labels and custom packaging each start at 300 pieces, with the application by model and artwork confirmed in the quote. Development of a new size or construction has separate requirements.

Link the drawing, material list, decorated sample, inspection record and packaging proof to one revision. Confirm quantities by SKU and costs for samples, setup, product, branding and shipping. Repeat supply starts at 50 pieces where the route permits; new branding or packaging can retain its 300-piece minimum. Request production or preparation time separately from transport, and agree artwork ownership and exclusivity terms for the project.
