Private-Label Wet Bags for First Period Kits

Build a practical kit around the items it needs to carry. This guide helps retail brands compare existing wet bags, period pouches and branded packaging before committing to a first order.

Prepared by ReuseCareNovember 27, 2026
Request a sample and itemised quote →

Plan a basic and premium assortment

A first-period care pack can use a small pouch to organise clean products and a separate wet bag for used reusable items or soiled clothing. Define these functions before selecting a colour palette. A pad pouch that fits in a pocket may be too small for a folded pair of period underwear, and a larger garment bag may be inconvenient for finding a clean pad. Ask the supplier to propose model references for each job.

Proposed accessory configurations to evaluate, not stocked retail kits
Planning optionAccessory configurationSample approval question
EssentialsOne clean-product pouch and one separate wet bagDo both bags fit the selected products and the intended school bag?
PremiumCoordinated pouch plus a larger or double-compartment wet bag, with branded packagingDoes the complete set provide useful extra capacity and clear storage instructions?

Write a bill of materials for each selling unit: model, colour, bag count, any pads or underwear included, insert, label and packaging. Essentials and Premium are proposed merchandising options. Confirm every item and quantity; a display photograph does not establish the contents of a supply offer. Link the selected reusable pad styles to their own size and material records.

Choose stock or a branded production route

Most ReuseCare products have ready-stock options. Existing styles can start at 50 pieces, subject to the selected SKU, colour and available quantity. Ask for current catalogue references and confirm whether an assortment can be mixed; a total order quantity alone does not confirm availability for every design.

Logo printing, woven branding labels and custom packaging each start at 300 pieces. Confirm how the minimum applies to the model, artwork and packaging version. A 50-piece stock order does not automatically include those custom services. New sizes, construction changes and special fabrics need a separate development and MOQ review. The wet bag MOQ and sample guide distinguishes these routes from the material-specific custom example.

Check fit and handling with real contents

Provide the largest intended folded pad, underwear and clothing dimensions. Load the sample with the actual planned contents, then check the opening clearance, closure operation, filled thickness and how easily each item is found and removed. Review both empty and filled bags inside the intended backpack or sports bag. Record finished dimensions and the agreed measurement method for each model.

For a two-compartment design, ReuseCare offers separate linings and openings for each compartment. Ask for the actual model and verify the divider, openings and usable space on a sample. The dual-compartment design checklist covers this review. Independent linings do not by themselves establish leakage resistance or prevent transfer between storage areas.

Approve branding and care information together

Compare pastel fabric swatches and printed proofs with an agreed physical colour reference. Specify logo placement from finished seams, the intended label location and readable care text. Confirm the printing method and its suitability for the chosen outer fabric and lining. Request a decorated sample and agree washing conditions and appearance criteria before approving a durability statement.

Keep instructions clear about the intended contents of each bag, temporary storage and the approved cleaning method. An intended school-age audience is not evidence of age-specific safety testing. Request documents applicable to the selected materials and product rather than relying on a generic certificate heading.

Sign off the whole kit and plan repeat supply

Approve numbered component samples and the assembled retail pack. Record the drawing revision, material codes, artwork files, contents, measurements, agreed checks and approval date. Resolve any fit or packaging change before bulk release. Existing reference photos illustrate product categories; they do not establish an approved branded kit.

Request comparable quotes at 50, 100, 250, 300, 500 and 1,000 pieces, with unavailable routes marked clearly. Separate product, decoration, labels, packaging, sampling, setup and freight. Ask for the preparation or production schedule and transport estimate separately. Use the RFQ checklist to send the same brief for each tier.

Repeat orders start at 50 pieces where the selected route permits. Fresh custom branding or packaging can still require its 300-piece minimum. Confirm retained artwork, stock availability, any repeat setup charge and whether replacement colours require a new sample. Agree artwork ownership, exclusivity scope, duration and fees case by case in the written order terms.
