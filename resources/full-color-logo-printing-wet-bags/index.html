Full-Colour Logo and Black Text on Wet Bags

Request a full-colour logo and separate black text on a washable waterproof wet bag, then judge both on a finished sample. This guide explains what to specify and approve; ReuseCare has not confirmed a full-colour production method for this configuration.

Published and updated November 21, 2026
Request a printed wet bag sample quote →

Send artwork the supplier can assess

Send an editable vector file or print-ready PDF with the logo and black copy on separate layers. Include an outlined production copy, the exact text in a separate file for proofreading, and a revision number. For raster elements, supply the original at final print size and ask the supplier to confirm the required resolution and colour profile. Flag gradients, fine rules, reversed lettering and trademark symbols that must survive at actual size.

Confirm that your licence covers reproduction on the bags, including any third-party icons or typefaces. State who can receive production files, whether an approved decorator may use them, and whether the supplier must delete or return them after the job. Share files privately for quotation and sampling; do not treat permission to manufacture as permission to publish the artwork.

Check the print method on the actual bag materials

Start with the wet bag construction: specify the outer fabric, its colour and finish, and the waterproof laminate. Ask whether the supplier would decorate the fabric before lamination, print an already laminated panel, or decorate the sewn bag. A method that looks good on a loose swatch can still distort the laminate, mark the surface or fail near a seam when used on the finished construction.

Ask for a feasibility assessment of any proposed digital, transfer or multi-pass process, including how it affects the fabric and laminate and whether logo and text can be printed in one operation. Require a finished sample made by the proposed route. The single-colour screen-printing guide documents a different specification; it does not establish feasibility for full colour.

Proof the logo, black copy and white ground together

Specify whether white means the actual outer fabric or a printed underbase. On white fabric, compare the fabric shade and opacity against the agreed reference; on a coloured ground, ask whether an underbase is needed and check for a white fringe around the print. For a white bag with a zipper, use the white zipper wet bag OEM checklist to specify the bag separately from the decoration.

Use a digital proof to approve spelling, artwork scale and layout, then compare a physical proof on the specified fabric with the buyer's colour reference under agreed lighting. Review black text at actual size and normal reading distance: check for filled letter openings, fuzzy edges, colour fringing and low contrast. Ask the supplier to recommend a minimum type and stroke size from the trial, then record the approved values.

Dimension the print box from the finished side seam and zipper or opening. Mark the viewing face, orientation and clearance from stitching, folds and tabs. Agree a measurable placement tolerance and inspect the empty bag flat and filled to its intended shape.

Agree wash and rub tests before sampling

Keep an unwashed control and test matching decorated bags. Record the wash cycle count, temperature, detergent, load and drying method against the proposed care instructions. Compare at agreed checkpoints after drying. For rubbing, use separate dry and damp white cloths on the logo and black text. Agree the stroke count, contact force and grading method beforehand, or have a lab perform an agreed rubbing test where repeatability matters.

Write the pass criteria before testing: readable text, no visible cracking or lifting, and colour change and white-cloth staining within agreed grades or approved reference limits. Inspect for laminate distortion or separation after washing, and agree a separate water-containment check on the finished bag if required. A readable print is not evidence that the seams or zipper are waterproof.

Sign off the sample and compare like-for-like tiers

Approve a labelled physical sample in writing. Record the artwork revision, actual fabric and laminate, proof reference, method, placement measurements, care conditions and test results. Keep an approved sample on each side for bulk comparison. Require a new approval if the decorator, material, colour or print process changes.

Request comparable quotes at 300, 500 and 1,000 pieces for the same construction and artwork. Ask which tiers are feasible and whether minimums apply per artwork or bag colour. Separate the base bag, artwork preparation, setup or tooling, underbase or extra print pass, sample and revision fees, testing, packaging and freight. Ask which charges recur on reorders and have sample and bulk lead times listed separately.

Use the custom wet bag RFQ checklist to complete the brief, then request a feasibility review and sample quote with your quantity tiers, bag specification and acceptance criteria.
