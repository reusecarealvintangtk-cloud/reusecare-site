Packaging and Warehouse Handover

Define the retail pack, variant count and carton information before a private-label order reaches final packing.

Prepared by ReuseCareUpdated September 29, 20267 minute read
Buyer takeaway

Packaging is a product specification. Pack contents, artwork revision, labels, barcodes, carton quantities and shipping marks should be approved before finished goods are packed.

Choose the selling unit first

Start by defining what the customer or distributor receives as one unit. A reusable menstrual pad set may contain several sizes and a wet bag. Nursing pads may be sold by pair or as a multi-pair set. Cloth diapers may include an insert, while a cover may be packed without one.

This definition controls the quotation, barcode, package dimensions, carton count and packing list. Write the unit clearly and avoid switching between pieces, pairs and sets in different documents.

Approve artwork by exact version

Packaging approval should identify the final artwork file, revision date and package structure. Check product name, material statements, care instructions, quantity, barcode, contact information, country-specific text and any required warning. Claims should be supported by documents that apply to the selected product.

Keep product and package variants connected

Mixed orders need a variant matrix. The matrix can connect each print, size or color to its retail artwork, barcode and carton quantity. This is especially important when several products use similar packaging but have different contents.

Selling unitPieces, pairs or sets, with every included component.
Artwork versionFinal file name, revision date and approval record.
Variant mappingPrint, color, size, barcode and pack relationship.
Carton ruleUnits per carton, marks, gross weight and dimensions.

Define carton information

Carton instructions commonly include buyer name or purchase order, product code, variant, quantity, carton number and destination marks. Confirm whether inner cartons, polybags, protective inserts or moisture controls are required. Shipping labels should not cover required carton information.

Count and reconcile before handover

The packing list should reconcile finished quantities by product and variant. If an order includes replacement pieces, samples or spare packaging, list them separately. Carton numbers help the warehouse and forwarder identify missing or damaged units without reopening every carton.

Protect the shipment record

Before dispatch, confirm the shipping method, delivery terms, collection details and required documents. “Dispatch within two days” for in-stock goods does not mean two-day delivery. Transit timing starts after the carrier receives the shipment and depends on the selected route.

Packaging brief checklist

  • One clear selling unit with every included product and accessory.
  • Final packaging structure, dimensions and material direction.
  • Approved artwork file, revision date, barcode and required text.
  • Variant matrix connecting products, packs and carton quantities.
  • Carton marks, carton numbering and destination information.
  • Packing list, shipping terms and handover documents.

Custom packaging minimums and lead times depend on the structure, print process and quantity. Confirm them separately from the product minimum.
