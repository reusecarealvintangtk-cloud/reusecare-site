OEM supplier selection

Private Label Period Underwear Manufacturer Buyer Guide

Build a manufacturing brief that lets you compare suppliers on the same fit, construction, branding and approval requirements.

Prepared by ReuseCareNovember 19, 2026
Request a sample or quote →Read the buyer guide

Define the first style before comparing manufacturers

Start with the person who will wear the garment and the role it will play in your range. Give the supplier a short description of intended use, preferred rise, front and back coverage, waistband feel and leg opening. Assign each requirement a priority. For example, back coverage might be fixed for your launch while the waistband finish is open to proposals. Give all bidders the same brief and ask them to identify deviations alongside their prices.

The photos show one style across colorways. Use the images on the period underwear page to point out visible details and use the filtered catalog to identify the reference you want quoted. Attach your own sketch or fit reference for any requested change. A common reference gives your merchandising team and the sample maker a shared starting point.

Make the fit brief measurable

Send your intended size range and a measurement chart if you have one. Specify where to place the tape for relaxed waist, hip, front rise, back rise and leg opening. Include how the garment should be laid out and whether a measurement is flat or around the body. Ask the supplier to propose tolerances and identify the base size for the first sample. Review fit and measurements on that sample before authorizing grading, then request size-set samples where grading or coverage needs checking.

Record feedback by measurement point and sample revision. If the back rides up in a fit session, record the sample size, the wearer feedback and the specific area to adjust. Ask the sample team to document how the change affects adjacent seams and coverage. Approving a revision from a new photo alone can leave the pattern change ambiguous.

Ask what each quotation includes

Request the proposed body fabric, waistband, gusset construction and care instructions in writing. Ask which details use the reference construction and which require development. If material declarations or certificates matter to the destination market, ask which documents cover the proposed garment or component and which evidence still needs review. Link each document to the style revision before signing off on label copy.

Provide artwork placement, preferred label format, packaging format and destination. Ask which files and color references the supplier needs to proof your branding. Request separate charges for development, branding setup and packaging where applicable. Compare quotes at the same quantities by size and color, and ask how changes in the mix affect minimums and timing. Specify the intended delivery point and clarify the freight assumptions in every offer.

Supplier comparison checklist

  • Confirm the sample reference, base size and pattern or construction changes included in the price.
  • Ask who supplies the measurement chart, who approves grading and how substitutions are documented.
  • Request sample fees, included revision rounds, project-specific minimums and the points that trigger each timeline.
  • Check that the quote names the label and packaging work included and states who approves artwork.
  • Assign owners for fit, testing and commercial approval, and record the evidence each person needs to sign off.

Move from inquiry to an approved reference

Send the brief through the period underwear sample and quote form. Ask for a response that identifies open questions and prices the agreed sample scope. Use the private-label process to brief branding, then agree on a separate gusset test protocol for any proposed performance wording.

When you receive the sample, record fit comments and artwork changes against its revision. Ask for a revised commercial offer if a change affects construction, labor or packaging. Before approving production, confirm which physical sample, measurement chart, material record and artwork proofs control the order. Use the sample approval guide to keep those references together and record any open issue that would delay sign-off.
