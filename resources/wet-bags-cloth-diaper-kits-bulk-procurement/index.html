Wet Bags for Cloth Diaper Kits: Bulk Buyer Guide

Specify the wet bag around the diapers, liners and change routine it needs to support. Give every supplier the same kit bill of materials and acceptance criteria.

Prepared by ReuseCareUpdated November 20, 2026

Public procurement context

Malta’s e-tendering portal lists a framework call covering reusable cloth nappies, nappy liners and wet bags, with a February 2026 tender deadline. It illustrates how a public buyer can source these categories together. See Malta’s official notice SPD6/2025/106.

Define the kit and its ordering unit

Use a bill of materials with one row per component and a revision for each kit variant. State whether the purchase order covers fully assembled kits or separately packed components for local assembly. For retail, define what goes in one sellable pack. For institutional distribution, define what goes to one recipient and list replenishment stock separately.

Order lineWhat to record
Cloth diapersPieces per kit by model and size. List absorbent inserts separately if supplied loose.
Nappy linersSheets per kit, or rolls per kit with sheets per roll. Record liner type and dimensions for the selected diaper.
Wet bagsPieces per kit by bag specification. List spare bags as a separate component quantity.
Finished kitsAssembled kits per variant and total. Multiply each component count by the matching kit quantity and add any separately ordered spares.

Identify the exact cloth diaper and nappy liner references before asking a supplier to check fit. Include the liner roll in the fit check if it ships inside the bag.

Check capacity and access with real contents

Specify what the bag carries after a change. List the maximum count of used diapers, their sizes, fitted inserts and how staff or caregivers fold them. Check fit with a representative damp load. Agree finished dimensions, usable opening width and tolerances. Ask the reviewer to load the bag, close it without forcing the closure and unload it without dragging the contents across the opening.

Ask whether clean diapers or unused liners must travel alongside used diapers. If so, ask whether the buyer requires separate wet and dry compartments or a separate pouch. Request a drawing of the divider and openings for any proposed dual-compartment design, then inspect both compartments loaded. Choose the closure around the access routine, including whether staff need to operate it with one hand.

Approve the finished bag before bulk production

Specify the outer textile, lining, seam construction and any binding that crosses from inside to outside. Use the matte TPU lining guide to frame material questions. Fabric beading describes what happens on a flat surface. Stitch holes, binding, corners and closure gaps need checks on the assembled sample.

Agree the number of bags to test, how wet the load should be, how long to hold it, and the orientations to check for the intended carry routine. Close each bag as instructed and place dry absorbent witness material at seams, corners and the opening. Record droplets and damp marks by location. Agree the pass criteria in advance, including whether any outward wicking or moisture transfer to a dry compartment is a failure.

Repeat the fit, closure and moisture checks after the agreed wash and drying cycles. Record the temperature, detergent and drying method. Check for lining separation, seam distortion and closure wear. Sign off a dated, revision-marked preproduction sample with its component references, test record and packed-kit photo.

Specify packing and traceability

State whether to pack the bag inside each kit or ship it separately. Approve the pack layout and label proof, including SKU, variant, component count, care instructions and required languages. Confirm kits or pieces per carton, carton dimensions, gross weight and carton markings. Ask how the packing list links each kit or carton lot to the component lots so receiving staff can isolate affected stock if a bag issue is found.

Comparable quote checklist

  • Require the same kit revision and quantities. Ask for pricing per assembled kit and separate component pricing where applicable.
  • Ask suppliers to list proposed substitutions and confirm loaded fit, construction and the sample test plan.
  • Itemize samples, validation, assembly, labels, packing and freight. Confirm currency, destination, delivery terms and exclusions.
  • Request milestones for sample approval, production and delivery, plus the packing and lot records supplied at dispatch.

Use the custom wet bag RFQ checklist for bag-specific fields and attach the kit bill of materials to the same request.
