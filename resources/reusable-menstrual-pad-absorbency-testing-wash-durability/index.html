Reusable Menstrual Pad Absorbency Testing and Wash Durability: Buyer Guide

For an absorbency or wash-durability claim, test the exact finished pad before and after a defined laundering schedule. Agree on how the lab will measure liquid intake, retention and leakage before samples arrive.

Identify and condition the test samples

A fabric water-beading photo shows how a particular surface responds to droplets at that moment. It cannot establish finished-pad absorbency. The contact layer, core, barrier, binding and needle holes all influence how liquid moves through an assembled pad. Even a backing that beads water can sit beneath a seam that wicks fluid onto underwear. Use the materials and layers guide to identify the construction you want the lab to test.

Assign each pad a specimen ID tied to its SKU, lot, size, pattern revision and material revision. Log the layer map, seam construction, wing shape and closure. Use multiple specimens from agreed lots and allocate comparable groups to baseline and washed testing so a previously wetted specimen does not affect the comparison. Specify whether baseline pads are tested as received or after the label’s first-use wash. Dry and condition both groups at the same agreed temperature, humidity and duration before weighing or measuring.

Run a controlled finished-pad challenge

Agree on a documented test fluid with the lab and record its formulation and temperature. Water can serve as a screening fluid, but its results cannot be treated as equivalent to those from a menstrual-fluid simulant. Fix the dose volume, delivery rate, application point, interval between doses and maximum cumulative volume. Mount the pad consistently on a defined fixture or reference underwear with the wings fastened. Use fresh specimens for separate center and off-center challenges so one exposure does not influence the next.

Finished-pad measurements to agree before testing
CheckMethod and record
IntakeTime each dose from the end of delivery until visible pooling clears, using a stated observation limit. Record runoff and doses that never clear.
Retention under loadAfter a fixed dwell, apply the agreed pressure for a set duration using a defined contact area. Record the pad’s mass gain over its conditioned dry mass after compression and weigh liquid transferred to a preweighed surface blotter separately.
Leakage and wickingUse a dry witness sheet beneath the assembly. Record the first dose and location where liquid reaches the sheet or escapes the pad edge. Check the underside, perimeter stitching, wing roots and snap areas before and after loading.

Repeat after the proposed laundering schedule

Choose wash checkpoints, including the last cycle you intend to substantiate. Define one cycle as the specified wash and complete dry. Record the wash method, temperature, detergent and dose, machine program or hand-wash procedure, load and drying conditions. Match the proposed care label and washing instructions. If the label permits multiple methods, decide which need separate evidence.

At each checkpoint, measure conditioned length, body width and wing span at marked reference points without stretching the pad. Report signed percentage change from each specimen’s baseline. Inspect for curling, opened seams, barrier delamination, snap damage and wing distortion. Run the same intake, retention and leakage checks on allocated specimens at the chosen checkpoints. A pad can keep its shape yet develop a leak path through stitching or release more liquid under the same load.

Set acceptance limits before seeing results

Agree on a maximum intake time, minimum retained mass, maximum surface transfer, leakage rule at the target cumulative dose and allowable dimensional change. Specify whether any leak, seam opening or closure failure rejects a specimen and how many specimens must pass at each checkpoint. Ask for individual readings and ranges alongside averages. A group mean can hide a single leaking pad. Document when a change in core, barrier, pattern or stitching triggers retesting.

Match label wording to traceable evidence

Request a dated lab report with its ID, method and revision, specimen and lot IDs, conditioning records, wash logs, fixture and load settings, raw measurements, photos of failure locations and any deviations. Check that the tested build matches the one you intend to order. Tie any capacity figure to its test endpoint and conditions, and any wash-count claim to the care method and the properties checked after washing. Avoid converting a fixture dose into hours of wear. Use the day and night selection guide to define the intended use of each variant before drafting performance language.

External references for buyer briefs

For US context, the FDA’s October 2025 publication is draft guidance on menstrual product performance testing and labeling. Its recommendations are nonbinding and the draft is not for implementation. FDA draft guidance.

UNICEF’s 2022 specification provides a program procurement benchmark that includes laundering and dimensional stability. Treat it as a reference when drafting tender criteria. It is neither a universal standard nor a ReuseCare product specification, approval or endorsement. UNICEF technical specifications for reusable menstrual pads.
